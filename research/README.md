# 100m Sprint Research Buckets

This folder organizes sprinting papers and applied takeaways by training topic.

Buckets:

- `sprint_training/`: acceleration, max velocity, sprint-specific endurance, technical sprint work.
- `strength_power/`: gym work, jump squats, squats, hip thrusts, strength-power tradeoffs.
- `resisted_sprinting/`: sled sprints, sled pushes, hills, resisted acceleration.
- `conditioning/`: tempo running, aerobic support, repeated-sprint ability, low-intensity days.
- `recovery_load_management/`: spacing, progression, intensity distribution, readiness.
- `injury_prevention/`: Nordic hamstring, hamstring risk reduction, eccentric work.
- `plyometrics/`: bounding, hopping, jumping on grass; no gym needed.
- `testing_measurement/`: measuring the ground, timing accuracy, pacing submaximal runs.

Use this as a working evidence library. Most entries are papers or reviews with links, DOI/PMID where available, and practical notes for your current 100m routine.

## Verification (2026-09-29)

- Every PMID and DOI was checked against PubMed or Crossref. All titles, authors, and years match, and none are fabricated.
- Key points were checked against each abstract, and against the full text for Haugen et al. 2019 and Van Hooren et al. 2024.
- Corrected entries: McBride 2002 (heavy jump squats slowed 20m sprint), Zhang 2022 (velocity-loss claim moved to Pareja-Blanco 2017), Van Hooren 2024 (3-4 exercises per session), Ward 2024 and Petrakos 2016 (resisted sprinting no better than normal sprinting), Cahill 2020 (force-velocity changes not significant), Ishøi 2018 (10m improved), Bishop and Girard 2011 (team-sport repeated-sprint research, weak fit for 100m tempo), Haugen 2019 (at least 48h; tempo 1000-2000m).
- Added the three sources in Gym_day_notes.txt that were missing here: Vanderka 2016 (jump squats), Young 2001 (RDL article), and Deane 2005 (hip flexion).
- Evidence strength by exercise:
  - Strongest: sprinting itself, squats and general strength, Nordics (for injury prevention), and plyometrics.
  - Moderate: jump squats (light), cleans, hip thrusts.
  - Weaker: RDL for sprint speed (its support is hamstring-architecture evidence), and hip flexion (one trial in untrained people).
  - Upper body: no sprint evidence; it is for general fitness.
