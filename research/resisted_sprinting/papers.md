# Resisted Sprinting Papers

## Petrakos et al. 2016

Title: Resisted Sled Sprint Training to Improve Sprint Performance: A Systematic Review

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26553497/

PMID: 26553497

DOI: 10.1007/s40279-015-0422-8

Type: Systematic review

Key points:

- Resisted sled sprinting is mainly useful for acceleration development.
- Load should match the goal: lighter loads preserve sprint mechanics; heavier loads target horizontal force but slow the movement more.
- Resisted sprint work should supplement, not replace, normal sprinting.
- The authors conclude that whether sled sprinting beats unresisted sprinting for acceleration or max velocity "remains equivocal".

Applied to your routine:

- If you can use a sled, hill, or safe sled-push setup, keep resisted work short: 10-20m.
- Pair it with free accelerations so mechanics stay fast.

## Ward et al. 2024

Title: Does Resisted Sprint Training Improve the Sprint Performance of Field-Based Invasion Team Sport Players? A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37897636/

PMID: 37897636

DOI: 10.1007/s40279-023-01952-8

Type: Systematic review and meta-analysis

Key points:

- 21 studies. Head-to-head, resisted sprinting was no better than normal sprinting for early acceleration, late acceleration, or max velocity.
- Within-group, resisted sprinting improved early acceleration (SMD -0.80) and late acceleration (SMD -0.28), but not max velocity.
- Light and very heavy loads both improved early acceleration.

Applied to your routine:

- Resisted work is optional. Plain 3-point accelerations on your ground get similar results, so having no sled or hill costs you little.
- If you do use it, put it on acceleration day, not tempo day, and do not turn it into conditioning.

## Cahill et al. 2020

Title: Influence of resisted sled-push training on the sprint force-velocity profile of male high school athletes

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31742795/

PMID: 31742795

DOI: 10.1111/sms.13600

Type: Intervention study

Key points:

- 50 high school athletes, 2 sessions per week for 8 weeks, at loads causing a 25%, 50%, or 75% velocity drop.
- 5-20m split times improved with all resisted loads, most in the first 5m and most with the heavy load. Unresisted sprinting did not improve significantly in this study.
- Force-velocity profile changes were small and not significant.

Applied to your routine:

- Your gym has no sled, so this does not apply for now. If you ever get access, keep sled pushes short and powerful.
- Hill sprints only work if there is a real slope nearby. A cricket ground is flat, so plain accelerations are the default (see Ward et al. 2024).

## Practical Resisted Sprint Options

Sled sprint:

- 4-6 x 10-20m.
- Rest 2-4 min.
- Load should let you stay aggressive without collapsing posture.

Hill sprint:

- 4-8 x 10-25m on a gentle hill.
- Walk-back plus extra rest.
- Good when no sled is available, but it needs an actual slope. A flat cricket ground does not provide one.

Contrast option:

- 2 rounds of 2 resisted 15m reps, then 1 free 20m sprint.
- Rest fully between reps.
