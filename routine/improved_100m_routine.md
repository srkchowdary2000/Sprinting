# Improved 100m Sprint Routine

This improves your current routine for both halves of your goal, a faster 100m and better overall athletic fitness, while respecting your setup: grass cricket ground, 100m diagonal with extra runway, limited gym access, and a current 100m best around 13s.

## Main Changes

- Stop making `4 x 100m max effort` the main sprint session every time.
- Split sprint work into acceleration, max velocity, and sprint-specific endurance.
- Keep tempo at 1000-2000m per session (your 6 x 300m is in range), but make it truly easy by timing the reps.
- Keep one focused gym day, but limit it to 3-4 main lower-body lifts.
- Add work that needs no gym: plyometrics after acceleration, and a short bodyweight strength block on a tempo day.
- Keep 48-72h between high-intensity sprint days, and about 72h between the gym day and the next sprint day.

## Venue Setup (One-Time)

Your ground: grass cricket ground, roughly 400m around (app-measured), with a diagonal of about 115-120m.

- Measure the 100m with a tape or measuring wheel. Even GPS sport watches were off by 3-6% in testing (Gilgen-Ammann et al. 2020), about 12-24m on a 400m lap, and phone apps are unlikely to do better.
- A 30m or 50m tape is enough: measure in segments along the exact line you run, keeping the tape taut and marking each segment end.
- Mark 0, 10, 20, 30, 60, and 100m with cones or landmarks, plus the flying zones for Session B.
- Put the start line at one end. A 3-point start needs no runway behind it, so all 15-20m of spare length becomes room to slow down after the finish.
- Straight-line limit: the longest max-effort rep on the diagonal is 100m. 120m sprints go on the boundary curve, which is gentler than a track bend. 150-300m tempo goes on the boundary or your U-path.
- If your line crosses the pitch square, shift it so the whole run stays on outfield grass.
- Before hard sessions, walk the lane for holes, sprinkler heads, and wet patches. On wet or dewy grass, swap max-effort 3-point starts for tempo or drills.
- The ground is closed on the 1st and 3rd weekends of each month, and you prefer weekdays, so all running is on weekdays. Only the gym is on the weekend.

## Weekly Template

You train 5 days a week: 4 weekdays at the ground and 1 gym day.

Day 1: Acceleration + short power (plyometrics)

Day 2: Tempo/recovery + bodyweight strength

Day 3: Max velocity or sprint-specific endurance

Day 4: Gym day

Day 5: Short easy tempo

Example week:

- Monday: Short easy tempo (about 1000m)
- Tuesday: Acceleration + plyometrics
- Wednesday: Main tempo (up to 2000m) + bodyweight strength block
- Thursday: Rest or mobility
- Friday: Max velocity or sprint-specific endurance
- Saturday: Gym
- Sunday: Rest

Why this order:

- Tuesday to Friday gives 72h between sprint days.
- Gym comes the day after a sprint day, as in Haugen's elite weekly template, and 72h before the next sprint day. Heavy squats need about that long to recover (Thomas et al. 2018).
- Tempo only needs 24h before the next hard session, so Monday and Wednesday are safe. Monday is the shorter, easier one because Tuesday is the first hard day of the week.
- If your gym day is Sunday instead, keep the same weekdays. The gap becomes 48h on either side of the gym, which is still acceptable.

In a busy week, drop Monday tempo first.

## Session A - Acceleration Day

Goal: improve start, first 10m, first 30m, and projection from 3-point start.

Warm-up:

- 8-12 min easy jog/walk and dynamic mobility.
- A-skips, B-skips, dribbles, ankling, high knees.
- 3 x 40m progressive build-ups at 60%, 70%, 80%.

Main work:

- 4 x 10m from 3-point start, rest 2 min.
- 4 x 20m from 3-point start, rest 3-4 min.
- 2 x 30m from 3-point start, rest 5 min.

Short power (after the sprints):

- Plyometrics on the grass, bodyweight only: pogo hops, broad jumps, alternate-leg bounds, single-leg hops.
- Weeks 1-2: about 55-60 foot contacts. Weeks 3+: 80-100.
- Full plan in `research/plyometrics/papers.md`.

Resisted work:

- Skipped. Your gym has no sled and the ground is flat. In a 21-study meta-analysis, resisted sprints were no better than plain sprints (Ward et al. 2024), so plain 3-point starts cover it.

Stop rule:

- Stop if starts become slow, posture collapses, or hamstrings feel tight.

## Session B - Max Velocity Day

Goal: improve top speed without the fatigue of repeated full 100m sprints.

Warm-up:

- Same dynamic warm-up as acceleration day.
- Include 3-4 progressive build-ups over 50-70m.

Main work:

- 3 x flying 20m with 30m build-up, rest 6-8 min.
- 2 x flying 30m with 30-40m build-up, rest 8-10 min.

How to set it up:

- Mark 30m build-up, then mark a 20m or 30m fly zone.
- Build smoothly, hit top speed before the fly zone, then sprint relaxed through the zone.
- The longest version (40m build-up + 30m fly = 70m) still leaves 45-50m of your diagonal to slow down.
- Haugen suggests less-trained athletes start with a 20m build-up into a 10m fly, so flying 10-20m is enough for weeks 1-2.

Technique cues:

- Tall posture.
- Fast ground contacts.
- Relax jaw, shoulders, and hands.
- Step down under hips; do not reach forward.

Stop rule:

- Stop if you start straining or your rhythm gets choppy.

## Session C - Sprint-Specific Endurance Day

Goal: maintain speed from roughly 60-100m and improve 100m finish.

Do this instead of max velocity day every other week, or after 2-3 weeks of acceleration/max velocity emphasis.

Warm-up:

- Same dynamic warm-up.
- 2 x 60m progressive build-ups.

Main work options:

- Option 1: 3 x 80m at 95-100%, rest 8-12 min.
- Option 2: 2-3 x 100m at 95-100%, rest 10-15 min. Haugen gives 2-3 x 100m with 10 min rest as a suitable session for less-trained athletes.
- Option 3: 2 x 120m at 95%, rest 12-15 min, run along the boundary curve. Your diagonal is too short for 120m plus room to slow down.

Important:

- Do not do 4 max-effort 100s every sprint day. Your old 4 x 100m (400m) fits Haugen's sprint-specific endurance range. It works as this session, just not as every sprint day.
- Quality matters more than volume.

## Session D - Tempo/Conditioning Day

Goal: build general fitness and promote recovery without damaging sprint quality.

Twice a week:

- Monday: the shorter, easier session, about 1000m (e.g. 10 x 100m), because acceleration day follows.
- Wednesday: the main session, up to 2000m (e.g. your 6 x 300m), followed by the bodyweight block.

Choose one (Haugen: 1000-2000m total per session):

- 10 x 100m at 60-70%, rest 60-90 sec (on the diagonal).
- 8 x 150m at 60-70%, rest 90 sec (boundary curve or U-path).
- 6 x 200m at 60-70%, rest 2 min (boundary curve or U-path).
- 5-6 x 300m at 60-65%, rest 2-3 min. This is your original U-path.

Pace targets (runners at your level run faster than intended when going by feel):

- 100m reps: about 18.5-21.5s, which is 60-70% of your 13s average race speed. Haugen measures tempo against top speed, which is faster than average race speed, so these targets err on the easy side.
- Longer reps: time the first rep, then keep every rep within 1-2s of it. If the last reps slow down, the first ones were too fast.

Rules:

- Tempo should feel easy enough that you could do more.
- Your U-path has room for a wide turn, so it is fine at tempo pace. Keep alternating clockwise and anticlockwise.
- If tempo affects your next sprint session, reduce volume first.

Bodyweight strength block (after Wednesday tempo, 15-20 min):

- Split squats or walking lunges: 2 x 8 each leg, bodyweight or a loaded backpack.
- Push-ups: 3 x 10-15.
- Band hip flexion: 2 x 10-15 each leg, with the band anchored to a fence or post (Deane et al. 2005).
- Side plank: 2 x 20-30s each side.
- This is your second strength day. WHO guidelines recommend muscle strengthening on 2 or more days a week, and the gym covers only one.

## Weekend Gym Day

Goal: strength and power with low wasted fatigue. Van Hooren et al. 2024 suggests 3-4 main exercises of 3-4 sets for sprinters, so the lower body is capped at 3-4 main lifts plus 2 low-volume sets of Nordics, and the rest rotates week to week.

Warm-up:

- 5-10 min general warm-up.
- Hip mobility, ankle mobility, glute activation.
- 2-3 low-intensity jumps.

Every week:

- Jump squat: 3-4 x 3-5, bodyweight to about 30% of squat 1RM, full rest. Heavy jump squats made athletes slower (McBride et al. 2002).
- Back squat or front squat: 3-4 x 3-5, 1-3 reps in reserve.
- Nordic curl: 2 x 3-6. Low volume works as well as high volume (Cuthbert et al. 2020).
- Weighted pull-ups or muscle-up work: 3 x 3-6.
- Dips: 3 x 6-10.

Week A adds:

- Romanian deadlift: 3 x 5-6.

Week B adds:

- Power clean: 3-4 x 2-3, if technique is safe. If not, the Tuesday plyometrics give a similar benefit (Morris et al. 2022).
- Hip thrust: 3 x 5-8.

Moved to the ground (no gym needed):

- Walking lunges or split squats, band hip flexion, and push-ups are now in the Wednesday bodyweight block.

If you miss a weekend:

- In soccer players, one gym session per week held strength and sprint gains, but one every other week lost them (Rønnestad et al. 2011). Do not miss two weekends in a row.
- For a missed week, do the Wednesday bodyweight block plus partner Nordics at the ground.

Do not do all exercises heavy in the same session. Prioritize in this order:

- Jump or clean.
- Squat.
- RDL or hip thrust.
- Nordic.
- Upper body.

## 4-Week Progression

Every week also includes Monday's short tempo (about 1000m). Skip it in week 4.

Week 1:

- Acceleration day + plyometrics (about 55-60 contacts).
- Tempo day + bodyweight block.
- Max velocity day (flying 10-20m).
- Gym day moderate (Week A).

Week 2:

- Acceleration day with slightly more 20-30m volume + plyometrics (about 55-60 contacts).
- Tempo day + bodyweight block.
- Sprint-specific endurance day with 3 x 80m.
- Gym day moderate (Week B).

Week 3:

- Acceleration day + plyometrics (80-100 contacts).
- Tempo day + bodyweight block.
- Max velocity day with flying 30s.
- Gym day moderate-heavy but no grinding (Week A).

Week 4:

- Reduced volume week.
- Acceleration day: 4 x 10m, 3 x 20m, and half the plyometric volume.
- Tempo day: 6 x 100m easy.
- Test day (Friday): 30m from a 3-point start, flying 20m, and 1-2 timed 100m attempts with full recovery. This also keeps your weekly max-velocity exposure, which lowered injury risk in Malone et al. 2017.
- Gym day light or skip heavy lower body.

## Testing

Test no more than every 3-4 weeks.

Useful tests:

- 30m from 3-point start.
- Flying 20m.
- 100m full sprint.

If you only time 100m, you will not know whether the improvement came from start, max speed, or endurance.

How to time it:

- Use the tape-measured, marked lane from Venue Setup.
- Best option: side-on slow-motion video with cones at the marks. A slow-motion-video iPhone app (MySprint) matched photocells within about 0.01s (Romero-Franco et al. 2017).
- With a stopwatch, single splits were off by about 0.15s on average (Hetzler et al. 2008). Treat changes under about 0.3s as possible noise, and use the same timer every time.
- Note wind, grass condition, and shoes. Grass times are slower than track times, so compare grass with grass.

## What To Remove From Current Routine

- Remove repeated `4 x 100m max effort` as the default workout.
- Remove back-to-back `B B` tempo days right before a sprint day. Two separate tempo days per week are fine and serve your fitness goal.
- Remove high-volume gym accessories when they cause soreness for sprint days.

## What To Keep

- Dynamic warm-up with skips.
- Long rest for true sprint reps.
- Static stretching after training if it feels good.
- 72h spacing after the hardest sprint sessions when needed. Research supports the 72h figure, but the fatigue is mostly muscular, not CNS (Thomas et al. 2018).
- Your 6 x 300m tempo volume on the U-path, run at a timed, truly easy pace.
- Grass for tempo. It is the surface elite sprint groups use.
