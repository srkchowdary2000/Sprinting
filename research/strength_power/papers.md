# Strength And Power Papers

## Seitz et al. 2014

Title: Increases in lower-body strength transfer positively to sprint performance: a systematic review with meta-analysis

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25059334/

PMID: 25059334

DOI: 10.1007/s40279-014-0227-1

Type: Systematic review and meta-analysis

Key points:

- Lower-body strength gains can transfer to sprint performance.
- Transfer is strongest when strength work is combined with sprint-specific training.
- Strength is useful, but it is not a substitute for high-quality sprinting.

Applied to your routine:

- Keep squats, Romanian deadlifts, hip thrusts, and lunges, but do not turn the gym day into bodybuilding volume.
- Use low-to-moderate volume and high intent so you recover for sprinting.

## Van Hooren et al. 2024

Title: Optimizing Resistance Training for Sprint and Endurance Athletes: Balancing Positive and Negative Adaptations

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11608172/

PMID: 39373864

DOI: 10.1007/s40279-024-02110-4

Type: Open-access review

Key points:

- Resistance training can improve sprint performance, but excessive hypertrophy can become a tradeoff.
- Sprint athletes should bias neural, strength, power, tendon, and architecture adaptations rather than chasing unlimited muscle mass.
- High-volume training to failure is not ideal for speed athletes.
- The paper's example dose for sprint athletes is 3-4 sets per exercise and 3-4 exercises per session: low volume, heavy, not to failure, and/or ballistic or plyometric sets.

Applied to your routine:

- Avoid taking every lower-body lift to failure.
- Keep the gym day to 3-4 main lower-body lifts. A 9-10 exercise gym day goes against this paper.
- Leave 1-3 reps in reserve on most strength sets.

## Neto et al. 2019

Title: Barbell Hip Thrust, Muscular Activation and Performance: A Systematic Review

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6544005/

PMID: 31191088

Type: Open-access systematic review

Key points:

- Hip thrusts strongly activate hip extensors, especially gluteus maximus.
- Acute hip-thrust potentiation can improve short sprint performance in some studies.
- Chronic sprint transfer is mixed, so hip thrusts are useful but not mandatory magic.

Applied to your routine:

- Keep hip thrusts as a secondary lift after squats or cleans.
- Use them for acceleration support: 3 x 4-6 or 3 x 6-8, not high-fatigue pump sets.

## McBride et al. 2002

Title: The effect of heavy- vs. light-load jump squats on the development of strength, power, and speed

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11834109/

PMID: 11834109

Type: Controlled trial

Key points:

- 8 weeks of jump squats at 30% vs 80% of squat 1RM in 26 athletic men.
- The 30% group improved peak power and velocity, increased 1RM, and trended toward faster 20m sprint times.
- The 80% group got stronger but ran significantly slower over 20m.

Applied to your routine:

- Keep jump squats light: bodyweight to about 30% of your squat 1RM (see Cormie et al. 2007 below). Heavy jump squats can make you slower.
- Put jump squats early in the gym session after warm-up, before heavy lifts.
- Use low reps with full rest: 3-5 sets of 3-5 reps.
- Stop if jump height or speed drops.

## Zhang et al. 2022

Title: The Role of Velocity-Based Training (VBT) in Enhancing Athletic Performance in Trained Individuals: A Meta-Analysis of Controlled Trials

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9368129/

PMID: 35954603

DOI: 10.3390/ijerph19159252

Type: Open-access meta-analysis

Key points:

- Velocity-based lower-body training improved strength, CMJ, and sprint time in trained males (9 studies, 253 men; sprint time SMD -0.40).
- The authors say the effect of velocity loss within a set still needs research. For that question, see Pareja-Blanco et al. 2017 below.

Applied to your routine:

- Even without a velocity tracker, use the idea: stop sets when bar speed clearly slows.
- Avoid grinding reps on sprint-focused gym days.

## Suchomel et al. 2016

Title: The Importance of Muscular Strength in Athletic Performance

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26838985/

PMID: 26838985

DOI: 10.1007/s40279-016-0486-0

Type: Review

Key points:

- Greater strength supports force production, power, jumping, and sprinting.
- Strength should be developed in a way that supports the sport skill.

Applied to your routine:

- Keep a heavy strength exposure weekly if possible.
- Since gym access is limited, one focused weekend session is useful, but it must not ruin the next sprint session.

## Vanderka et al. 2016

Title: Improved Maximum Strength, Vertical Jump and Sprint Performance after 8 Weeks of Jump Squat Training with Individualized Loads

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4974862/

PMID: 27803628

Type: Controlled trial (this is the first jump squat link in your Gym_day_notes.txt)

Key points:

- 68 moderately trained men; jump squats 3 times per week for 8 weeks, at loads that kept every rep at 90% or more of maximum power, with real-time feedback.
- 50m sprint time improved about 1% by mid-training and a further 1.9% by the end; CMJ, squat jump, and maximal force also improved.
- Controls did not improve on sprint or jumps.

Applied to your routine:

- Supports keeping jump squats. Stopping sets before power drops is the same idea as velocity-based training.
- The study trained 3 times a week; you can do the gym version once a week and add bodyweight jumps on a ground day.

## Cormie et al. 2007

Title: Optimal loading for maximal power output during lower-body resistance exercises

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17277599/

PMID: 17277599

DOI: 10.1249/01.mss.0000246993.71599.bf

Type: Acute loading study (12 Division I male athletes)

Key points:

- Jump squat peak power was highest at 0% of 1RM (bodyweight only). Power was significantly lower from about 42% of 1RM upward (from 27% when expressed relative to body mass).
- Squat power peaked around 56% of 1RM but did not differ much across loads; power clean peaked at 80% of 1RM.

Applied to your routine:

- Bodyweight jump squats are already a peak-power stimulus, so they can be done on the grass ground with no gym.
- In the gym, keep jump squats light; use power cleans for heavier explosive loading if your technique is solid.

## Deane et al. 2005

Title: Effects of hip flexor training on sprint, shuttle run, and vertical jump performance

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16095411/

PMID: 16095411

DOI: 10.1519/14974.1

Type: Controlled trial

Key points:

- 8-week hip flexion resistance program in physically active but untrained men and women (24 trained, 24 controls).
- Hip flexion strength +12.2%, 40-yard dash time -3.8%, shuttle run time -9.0%; no change in controls.

Applied to your routine:

- This is most likely the study behind the elastic-tubing figure in your notes (the ResearchGate page could not be opened to confirm).
- Band hip flexion needs no gym: anchor a band to a fence or post at the ground.
- The subjects were untrained, so expect smaller gains; keep it as a short accessory.

## Young et al. 2001

Title: Resistance Training for Short Sprints and Maximum-speed Sprints

Link: https://doi.org/10.1519/00126548-200104000-00001

DOI: 10.1519/00126548-200104000-00001

Type: Practitioner article in Strength and Conditioning Journal 23(2), not a trial or systematic review (this is the "Romanian DL literature" link in your notes)

Key points:

- Coaching-oriented discussion of resistance exercise selection for acceleration and maximum speed.
- It is expert opinion, so it is weaker evidence than the reviews in this folder.

Applied to your routine:

- For the RDL, the stronger evidence is Bourne et al. 2017 in `injury_prevention/` and the deadlift EMG finding in Neto et al. 2019 above.

## Contreras et al. 2017

Title: Effects of a Six-Week Hip Thrust vs. Front Squat Resistance Training Program on Performance in Adolescent Males: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27253835/

PMID: 27253835

DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001510

Type: Randomized controlled trial

Key points:

- Hip thrust training showed potentially beneficial effects on 10m and 20m sprint times compared with front squats.
- Front squats were better for vertical jump and front squat strength.

Applied to your routine:

- The hip thrust has a reasonable case for acceleration; squats cover vertical power. Rotating both across weeks is sensible.
- The subjects were adolescents, so treat this as supporting evidence, not proof.

## Morris et al. 2022

Title: Comparison of Weightlifting, Traditional Resistance Training and Plyometrics on Strength, Power and Speed: A Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9213388/

PMID: 35025093

DOI: 10.1007/s40279-021-01627-2

Type: Systematic review and meta-analysis (16 studies, 427 participants)

Key points:

- Compared with traditional resistance training, weightlifting improved CMJ more (g = 0.95) and showed a large but non-significant effect on sprint speed.
- Weightlifting and plyometrics produced similar improvements in sprint speed, power, and strength.
- Heterogeneity and risk of bias were high.

Applied to your routine:

- Cleans are worth keeping if your technique is good.
- Plyometrics give similar speed and power benefits without a gym, so they are the fallback when the clean is shaky or gym time is short.

## Pareja-Blanco et al. 2017

Title: Effects of velocity loss during resistance training on athletic performance, strength gains and muscle adaptations

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27038416/

PMID: 27038416

DOI: 10.1111/sms.12678

Type: Randomized training study

Key points:

- Squat training that stopped each set at 20% velocity loss gave similar strength gains to 40% velocity loss, with 40% fewer reps.
- The 20% group improved CMJ more (9.5% vs 3.5%) and kept its fast type IIX fiber share; the 40% group gained more quadriceps size and lost IIX share.

Applied to your routine:

- End sets when reps slow noticeably. This is the source for that rule (it was previously credited to Zhang et al. 2022).

## Rønnestad et al. 2011

Title: Effects of in-season strength maintenance training frequency in professional soccer players

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21873897/

PMID: 21873897

DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0b013e31822dcd96

Type: Controlled training study (small groups, n = 7 each)

Key points:

- After 10 weeks of twice-weekly lifting, one session per week maintained strength and 40m sprint gains for 12 weeks.
- One session every second week led to losses in leg strength and 40m sprint time.

Applied to your routine:

- One weekend gym day is enough to hold your strength. Do not let it slip to every other week.
- Once a week maintains more than it builds. Haugen et al. 2019 notes elite sprinters lift 2-3 times per week, so add a short no-gym strength day at the ground.
