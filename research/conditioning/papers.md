# Conditioning Papers

## Bishop et al. 2011

Title: Repeated-sprint ability - part II: recommendations for training

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21846163/

PMID: 21846163

DOI: 10.2165/11590560-000000000-00000

Type: Review

Key points:

- Repeated-sprint ability (RSA) means repeating sprints of 10s or less with 60s or less of recovery. It is a team-sport quality.
- The review's two recommendations: train single-sprint speed plus strength/power, and add some high-intensity interval training (80-90% VO2max) to improve recovery between sprints.
- It does not study 100m sprinters or tempo running.

Applied to your routine:

- Weak fit for a 100m goal: it neither supports nor rules out your tempo sessions. Your tempo guidance comes from Haugen et al. 2019 below.
- For the "overall athletic fitness" half of your goal, it is a reason to keep a real conditioning day rather than cutting it.

## Girard et al. 2011

Title: Repeated-sprint ability - part I: factors contributing to fatigue

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21780851/

PMID: 21780851

DOI: 10.2165/11590550-000000000-00000

Type: Review

Key points:

- Sprint repetition fatigue involves central and peripheral factors.
- Phosphocreatine recovery, metabolic disturbance, and neuromuscular fatigue affect repeated sprint quality.
- The studies used team-sport protocols with short recoveries (under 60s).

Applied to your routine:

- This explains why short rests ruin max-effort reps, which supports your long rests between sprints.
- Do not let conditioning days compromise the next high-quality sprint day.

## Haugen et al. 2019 Tempo Guidance

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6872694/

Key points:

- Tempo running is 100-300m at 60-70% of maximal sprint velocity, with 1-3 min recovery and 1000-2000m total per session (Haugen Table 2).
- The table allows 24h before the next high-intensity session and lists "trainers on grass" as the footwear and surface.
- Haugen notes the evidence that tempo speeds recovery is limited. Its value is extra training volume, long-term durability, and cardiovascular fitness.

Applied to your routine:

- Your original 6 x 300m (1800m) is inside this range, and your grass ground is the surface elite groups use for tempo.
- Keep sessions at 1000-2000m, for example 10 x 100m, 8 x 150m, 6 x 200m, or 5-6 x 300m. Control the intensity rather than cutting the volume.
- Running the boundary is fine. A roughly round 400m ground has a radius of about 64m, a much gentler curve than a track bend (about 36.5m). Alternating direction, as you already do, balances the load on each leg.

## Practical Conditioning Rules

- Use tempo as a recovery and general-fitness tool.
- Keep intensity conversational: around 60-70%, not 80%.
- Time the reps instead of going by feel. Runners at your level ran faster than the effort they were told to use (Tillaar et al. 2025, in `testing_measurement/`).
- Prefer straight or gently curved routes. A wide U-turn with plenty of room, like the one on your U-path, is fine at tempo pace; only tight, sharp turns are a problem.
- Stop if your calves, hamstrings, or hip flexors feel more beaten up than after sprint days.

## Bull et al. 2020 (WHO Guidelines)

Title: World Health Organization 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7719906/

PMID: 33239350

DOI: 10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955

Type: International guideline

Key points:

- Adults: 150-300 min of moderate or 75-150 min of vigorous aerobic activity per week, or an equivalent mix.
- Adults should also do muscle-strengthening activities at moderate or greater intensity, involving all major muscle groups, on 2 or more days a week.

Applied to your routine:

- This covers the "overall athletic fitness" half of your goal. Sprint sessions are mostly rest between reps, so two tempo days per week do most of the aerobic work.
- One gym day is below the 2-day strength guideline. A short bodyweight strength block on a ground day fixes this without extra gym access.
