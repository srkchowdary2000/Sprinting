# Plyometrics Papers

## Sáez de Villarreal et al. 2012

Title: The effects of plyometric training on sprint performance: a meta-analysis

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22240550/

PMID: 22240550

DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0b013e318220fd03

Type: Meta-analysis (26 studies, 56 effect sizes)

Key points:

- Bigger sprint gains came from programs lasting under 10 weeks, with at least 15 sessions and more than 80 jumps per session at high intensity.
- Mixing jump types, and including horizontal jumps and sprint-specific drills, beat using only one jump type.
- Adding weight to plyometrics gave no extra benefit.

Applied to your routine:

- Use a mix of bounds, hops, and broad jumps, with horizontal jumps emphasized.
- Bodyweight only: no vest or dumbbells needed.
- Plyometrics once a week plus jump squats on gym day gives about 2 explosive jump sessions per week.

## Impellizzeri et al. 2008

Title: Effect of plyometric training on sand versus grass on muscle soreness and jumping and sprinting ability in soccer players

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17526621/

PMID: 17526621

DOI: 10.1136/bjsm.2007.038497

Type: Randomized trial (37 soccer players, 4 weeks)

Key points:

- Plyometrics on grass improved 10m and 20m sprint and jump performance. Sprint gains were the same on grass and sand.
- Grass improved CMJ more than sand; sand caused less soreness.

Applied to your routine:

- Your grass cricket ground is a tested surface for plyometric training.

## Haugen et al. 2019 Plyometric Guidance

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6872694/

Key points:

- Science and best practice are in "good agreement" that sprinters should use bounding, hopping, jumping, and skipping variations.
- Emphasize leg stiffness (short ground contacts) and horizontal power.
- Common drills: hurdle jumps, multi-jump circuits, medicine ball throws, and single-leg bounding.
- In the elite weekly template (Table 3), plyometrics sit on the days after sprint sessions, and some plyometric work continues in-season.

Applied to your routine:

- Plyometrics need no gym, so they are the best way around your limited gym access.
- Put them after the acceleration reps on acceleration day. That keeps explosive work on one hard day and leaves 72h before max velocity day.

## Practical Plyometric Plan (Grass Ground)

Where: after the acceleration reps on acceleration day. Count every foot landing as one contact.

Weeks 1-2 (about 55-60 contacts):

- Pogo hops (stiff ankles, quick contacts): 3 x 10.
- Standing broad jump: 5 x 1, reset between jumps.
- Alternate-leg bounds: 2 x 20m.

Weeks 3+ (about 80-100 contacts):

- Pogo hops: 3 x 10.
- Standing broad jump: 3 x 3, continuous.
- Alternate-leg bounds: 3 x 20-30m.
- Single-leg hops: 2 x 5 each leg.

Rules:

- Rest 1-2 min between sets. Every contact should be quick and springy.
- Stop when ground contacts get long or form breaks.
- No added weight.
- Bodyweight jump squats can replace broad jumps on some days.
- Skip bounding on wet, slippery grass.
