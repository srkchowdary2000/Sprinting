# Sprint Training Papers

## Haugen et al. 2019

Title: The Training and Development of Elite Sprint Performance: an Integration of Scientific and Best Practice Literature

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6872694/

PMID: 31754845

DOI: 10.1186/s40798-019-0221-0

Type: Open-access review

Key points:

- 100m performance depends on acceleration, maximal velocity, and sprint-specific endurance.
- Elite sprint practice separates session intent instead of repeatedly running full 100m all-out.
- Best-practice ranges: acceleration 10-50m at >98%, max velocity flying 10-30m at >98%, sprint-specific endurance 80-150m at >95%, tempo 100-300m at 60-70%.
- Total volume per session (Table 2): acceleration 100-300m, max velocity 50-150m (not counting the build-up), sprint-specific endurance 300-900m, tempo 1000-2000m.
- Intensive sprint sessions need at least 48h before the next one (Table 2: 48h after acceleration, 48-72h after max velocity and sprint-specific endurance).
- Training is polarized: at or above 95% of max velocity, or below 70%. The paper calls the zone in between a possible "black hole".
- For less-trained athletes, 2-3 x 100m with 10 min rest is an adequate sprint-specific endurance session, and flying sprints can use a 20m build-up into a 10m fly with about 4 min rest.
- All high-intensity rows in Table 2 assume spikes on a track; tempo assumes trainers on grass.

Applied to your routine:

- Replace frequent `4 x 100m max effort` with targeted acceleration, fly sprint, and sprint-endurance sessions.
- Your original 4 x 100m with 10+ min rest (400m) is a valid sprint-specific endurance session by these numbers. The problem was using it for every sprint day, not the session itself.
- Keep long rests, but match them to the goal: 2-7 min for short acceleration, 4-15 min for flying sprints, 8-30 min for sprint endurance.
- Stop a session when times drop or technique clearly degrades.
- On grass, your times will be slower than on a track, so compare only with your own grass times.

## Rumpf et al. 2016

Title: Effect of Different Sprint Training Methods on Sprint Performance Over Various Distances: A Brief Review

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26492101/

PMID: 26492101

DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001245

Type: Review

Key points:

- 48 studies, 1485 subjects. Specific sprint training had the largest effects (ES -1.00, about -3.2%), and they were largest over the 31m+ section.
- Nonspecific training (resistance and plyometrics) had its biggest effect over 31m+ (ES -0.43). Combined specific and nonspecific training had its biggest effect over 0-10m (ES -0.59).
- Sprinting is the most effective method; strength and plyometrics add to it.

Applied to your routine:

- Use a weekly spread of sprint qualities instead of one generic max-effort 100m day.
- Track split zones if possible: 0-30m, 30-60m, 60-100m. This shows whether your problem is start/acceleration, top speed, or speed maintenance.
- Plyometrics are one of the reviewed methods, and the routine had none. See `plyometrics/`.

## Practical Sprint Session Ranges

These ranges come mainly from Haugen et al. and are useful for your grass-ground setup.

Acceleration:

- 4-8 reps of 10-30m from 3-point start.
- Rest 2-5 min.
- Keep all reps crisp and powerful.

Max velocity:

- 3-5 reps of 20m flying sprints.
- Use 20-40m build-up, then sprint through a marked 20m zone.
- Rest 5-10 min.

Sprint-specific endurance:

- 2-4 reps of 80-120m at 95-100%.
- Rest 8-15 min.
- This is closer to your current 100m max day, but with lower volume and clearer quality control.
- Your diagonal is about 115-120m, so the longest max-effort straight rep is 100m with room to slow down. Reps over 100m go on the boundary curve.

Tempo/recovery:

- 100-300m reps at 60-70%, enough for 1000-2000m total.
- Rest 1-3 min.
- Should leave you fresher, not drained.
