# Injury Prevention Papers

## Al Attar et al. 2017

Title: Effect of Injury Prevention Programs that Include the Nordic Hamstring Exercise on Hamstring Injury Rates in Soccer Players: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27752982/

PMID: 27752982

DOI: 10.1007/s40279-016-0638-2

Type: Systematic review and meta-analysis

Key points:

- Programs including Nordic hamstring exercise reduced hamstring injury rates in soccer players.
- Evidence is strongest for injury prevention, not necessarily direct 100m performance.

Applied to your routine:

- Keep Nordics, but dose them carefully. They can create major soreness.
- Do them after sprinting or on gym day, not before sprinting.

## Bautista et al. 2021

Title: The effects of the Nordic hamstring exercise on sprint performance and eccentric knee flexor strength: A systematic review and meta-analysis of intervention studies among team sport players

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33893033/

PMID: 33893033

DOI: 10.1016/j.jsams.2021.03.009

Type: Systematic review and meta-analysis

Key points:

- Nordics improve eccentric knee-flexor strength (SCMD 0.83), with less consistent gains in well-trained players.
- Sprint effects are small: -0.04s overall, and -0.06s over 10m (significant). 5m and 20m effects were not significant.
- The included studies had some concerns or a high risk of bias.

Applied to your routine:

- Use Nordics as insurance and posterior-chain capacity work.
- Keep volume low: 2-3 sets of 3-6 reps.

## Ishoi et al. 2018

Title: Effects of the Nordic Hamstring exercise on sprint capacity in male football players: a randomized controlled trial

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29192837/

PMID: 29192837

DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2017.1409609

Type: Randomized controlled trial

Key points:

- 10 weeks of Nordics improved best 10m sprint time by 0.047s (d = 0.64) and peak eccentric hamstring strength (d = 0.92) versus controls.
- Total repeated-sprint time trended better (p = 0.056). 10 of 35 players were lost to follow-up.
- Useful evidence for hamstring preparation, but football players are not identical to dedicated 100m sprinters.

Applied to your routine:

- Progress Nordics slowly.
- If Nordics make you sore for sprint days, reduce volume immediately.

## van Dyk et al. 2019

Title: Including the Nordic hamstring exercise in injury prevention programmes halves the rate of hamstring injuries: a systematic review and meta-analysis of 8459 athletes

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30808663/

PMID: 30808663

DOI: 10.1136/bjsports-2018-100045

Type: Systematic review and meta-analysis

Key points:

- 15 studies across multiple sports: programs including Nordics cut the hamstring injury risk ratio to 0.49.
- The effect held in randomized trials only (0.52) and after removing high-risk-of-bias studies (0.55).

Applied to your routine:

- Broader than Al Attar et al. 2017 (soccer only), so it is the stronger reason to keep Nordics.

## Cuthbert et al. 2020

Title: The Effect of Nordic Hamstring Exercise Intervention Volume on Eccentric Strength and Muscle Architecture Adaptations: A Systematic Review and Meta-analyses

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6942028/

PMID: 31502142

DOI: 10.1007/s40279-019-01178-7

Type: Systematic review and meta-analyses

Key points:

- Over 6 weeks or more, low-volume Nordic programs produced eccentric strength and fascicle-length gains similar to high-volume programs.

Applied to your routine:

- This supports the low-volume plan below (2-3 sets of 3-6, once per week). More volume mainly adds soreness.

## Bourne et al. 2017

Title: Impact of the Nordic hamstring and hip extension exercises on hamstring architecture and morphology: implications for injury prevention

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27660368/

PMID: 27660368

DOI: 10.1136/bjsports-2016-096130

Type: Controlled training study (30 recreationally active men, 10 weeks)

Key points:

- Nordics and a hip-extension exercise both lengthened biceps femoris long head fascicles by a similar amount.
- Hip-extension training grew the biceps femoris long head more than Nordics did.

Applied to your routine:

- Knee-dominant work (Nordic) and hip-dominant work (RDL-type hinge) complement each other, so keep both.
- The study's exercise was a loaded hip extension, not an RDL specifically, but it is the same hip-dominant family.

## Malone et al. 2017

Title: High chronic training loads and exposure to bouts of maximal velocity running reduce injury risk in elite Gaelic football

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27554923/

PMID: 27554923

DOI: 10.1016/j.jsams.2016.08.005

Type: Prospective cohort (37 elite Gaelic footballers, one season)

Key points:

- Players who reached more than 95% of max velocity in training had lower injury risk than those who only reached 85% (OR 0.12).
- Too little and too much max-velocity exposure both raised risk (a U-shaped relationship).

Applied to your routine:

- A weekly max-velocity exposure is protective, not just risky. Do not drop fast running for weeks at a time, even in reduced-volume weeks.

## Practical Hamstring Plan

Weeks 1-2:

- 2 x 3 Nordics, once per week.

Weeks 3-4:

- 2 x 4-5 Nordics, once per week.

Weeks 5+:

- 2-3 x 4-6 Nordics, once per week.

Rules:

- Never do hard Nordics before max velocity sprinting.
- Stop reps before form collapses.
- Pair Nordics with RDLs only if soreness is manageable.
- Nordics need no gym: a partner holds your ankles, or hook your heels under something fixed. On grass, kneel on a folded towel or mat.
