# Testing And Measurement Papers

## Gilgen-Ammann et al. 2020

Title: Accuracy of Distance Recordings in Eight Positioning-Enabled Sport Watches: Instrument Validation Study

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7381051/

PMID: 32396865

DOI: 10.2196/17118

Type: Instrument validation study

Key points:

- 8 GPS sport watches had mean absolute distance errors of 3.2-6.1%. Running produced larger errors than walking or cycling.
- Distances were often underestimated, by up to 9%.

Applied to your routine:

- Your ~400m circumference comes from a phone running app, which is unlikely to beat a dedicated sport watch. A 3-6% error is 12-24m on 400m.
- Your "100m" diagonal is therefore an estimate. Measure it once with a measuring tape or wheel, then mark it.

## Hetzler et al. 2008

Title: Reliability and accuracy of handheld stopwatches compared with electronic timing in measuring sprint performance

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18978613/

PMID: 18978613

DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0b013e318185f36c

Type: Measurement study

Key points:

- Stopwatch times averaged only 0.04-0.05s off electronic timing, but individual splits were off by 0.15s on average (SD 0.20s).
- Stopwatch splits were faster than electronic in 67% of cases. The error was too scattered to correct with a fixed number.

Applied to your routine:

- Your 17s to 13s improvement is far bigger than timing error. Future gains of 0.1-0.3s are not.
- Treat a change of less than about 0.3s between two stopwatch-timed runs as possible noise. Do not "correct" hand times to electronic times.

## Romero-Franco et al. 2017

Title: Sprint performance and mechanical outputs computed with an iPhone app: Comparison with existing reference methods

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27806673/

PMID: 27806673

DOI: 10.1080/17461391.2016.1249031

Type: Validation study (12 highly trained sprinters, 40m sprints)

Key points:

- A high-speed-video iPhone app (MySprint) matched timing photocells for split times (r = 0.989-0.999, error about 0.01s).
- It also matched a radar gun on force-velocity profile values (F0, V0, Pmax).

Applied to your routine:

- Side-on slow-motion video with cones at your split marks gives near-photocell timing on a grass ground.
- The validated app is iPhone-only. On another phone, counting slow-motion frames between marks uses the same principle, but this study did not test that.

## Tillaar et al. 2025

Title: Relationships Between Effort, Rate of Perceived Exertion, and Readiness During a Warm-Up for High-Speed Sprinting

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12101144/

PMID: 40407439

DOI: 10.3390/jfmk10020155

Type: Test-retest study (19 adults, mean 100m PB 13.07s)

Key points:

- In a warm-up of 8 x 50m rising from 60% to 95% effort, runners' actual speed was generally higher than prescribed, especially on the first test day.
- Alignment improved with practice, except at 80-90%.
- The progressive warm-up left runners ready for a maximal 50m sprint.

Applied to your routine:

- These runners were at your level (about 13s), so your "60%" tempo and "70-80%" build-ups are probably faster than you think.
- Use target times for tempo reps rather than feel.
- Supports the progressive build-ups in the routine's warm-up.

## Haugen et al. 2019 Testing Note

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6872694/

Key points:

- For elite sprinters, year-to-year changes are smaller than normal day-to-day variation and than the effects of wind and timing method.

Applied to your routine:

- As your improvements get smaller, conditions start to matter. Note wind direction, grass condition, shoes, and time of day for every test.

## Practical Testing Setup

- Measure the 100m once with a tape or measuring wheel. Put cones at 0, 10, 20, 30, 60, and 100m, plus the flying-sprint zones.
- Start at one end so all the spare length is after the finish for slowing down.
- Time with side-on slow-motion video, or at least use the same person, stopwatch, and start signal every time.
- Test in the same direction on similar grass. Prefer calm days, and always note the wind.
- Test every 3-4 weeks: 30m from a 3-point start, flying 20m, and 100m.
